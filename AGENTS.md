# Product guardrail

Before implementing a proposed feature, assess it against mySwamp's core purpose and product language. Explicitly warn the user and give reasoned pushback when a feature would add friction to the one-frog experience, turn mySwamp into a generic task manager, weaken its calm/minimal character, confuse the frog/tadpole/water model, or damage the quality of data needed for future Deep Swamp analysis. Recommend the narrowest aligned alternative instead of silently implementing a conflicting request.

# Visual language

- mySwamp is matte, low-contrast, calm, and spatial. Avoid glossy treatments, bright white outlines, conspicuous glow, glass effects, and generic premium-SaaS polish.
- Prefer wide, landscape-like surfaces that echo the main task water over tall, narrow settings-style modals.
- Use hierarchy through proportion, depth, waterlines, negative space, and selective motion—not by making every control equally prominent.
- Organic does not mean hand-drawn or quirky. Use restrained asymmetry, softly irregular silhouettes, invasive branches, a few leaves, lilypads, and water responses while keeping controls precise and legible.
- Avoid grids of identical manufactured pills where possible. Choice groups may wrap naturally, and selection should feel like a quiet disturbance in water rather than a shiny state change.
- Keep labels compact, lower-case, and non-redundant. Remove explanatory copy when layout and interaction already communicate the idea.
- New screens should feel like places within the same swamp, not polished components placed on top of it.
